Sports

Manipur gave the world polo and gives India more than its share of champions. Sport here is history, festival and everyday life at once.

Meet the champions

Polo and football

Sagol Kangjei

The game the British called polo has been played in Manipur for centuries, on small, sure-footed Manipuri ponies. Officers stationed in the region learned it in the 1850s and carried it to Calcutta, then the world.

Imphal still hosts tournaments, and the Manipuri pony is protected as a breed of its own.

Grounds of kings

Manung Kangjeibung, inside Kangla, was the polo ground of the royal court. Mapal Kangjeibung in central Imphal is counted among the oldest polo grounds still in use anywhere.

Football country

Football is played on every open field. Manipur sends players to the national team and to clubs across India, and local league matches draw loud, loyal crowds.

Traditional games

Many are played at Yaoshang and at harvest, and some only at certain festivals.

  1. On horseback

    Sagol Kangjei

    Polo, played seven a side in its traditional form.

  2. Team game

    Yubi Lakpi

    A greased coconut is carried through the opposing side, a little like rugby.

  3. Wrestling

    Mukna

    Manipuri wrestling, with holds on the belt; matches end when a back touches the ground.

  4. On water

    Hiyang Tannaba

    Long-boat races rowed in the moats of Kangla and Bijoy Govinda.

  5. Martial art

    Thang-Ta

    Sword and spear, taught in academies and performed on stage.

A state of champions

For a state of about three million people, Manipur has won a remarkable share of India’s medals.

M. C. Mary Kom

Boxing. Six-time world amateur champion and Olympic bronze medallist at London 2012.

Saikhom Mirabai Chanu

Weightlifting. Olympic silver at Tokyo 2020 and world champion in 2017.

Ngangom Dingko Singh

Boxing. Asian Games gold in Bangkok, 1998, an inspiration for a generation.

Laishram Sarita Devi

Boxing. World champion in 2006 and Asian champion several times.

N. Kunjarani Devi

Weightlifting. A string of world championship medals and Commonwealth gold in 2006.

Sushila Chanu

Hockey. Captain of India’s women’s team at the Rio 2016 Olympics.
